## Observed generic failure mode

A project's early design-direction brief may identify concrete typography, palette or utility-icon selection as **OPEN**. Later effective sources may establish concrete choices while the earlier brief's unchanged present-tense wording still says they are open. A reader or agent can then misinterpret the standing and reopen decisions already resolved by the legitimate owner.

A narrow correction to the old source's **status and owning-source pointers** can resolve that conflict without a second brand exploration, visual redesign or master standards registry.

## Source organization lesson

An index may mix **canonical rules**, working supplemental advice, direction-only visual evidence and feature-specific design candidates. A concise project-owned authority map and `Owns / Does not own` boundaries in existing canonical documents can clarify routing and standing **without restructuring the folder or adding mandatory categories**.

This is a *reusable decision pattern*, not proof that any given design system has passed real browser, accessibility, usability, independent Engineering consumer or production tests.

## Explicit non-transfer

Do not infer a standard count, fixed visual system, specific fonts/colors, component-library requirement or operational branch acceptance for a different project. A legitimate target project remains the only source of its actual Design Standard rules and their Human approval.
